## 1. Main finding
The Russia–Ukraine energy war has turned asymmetric. Facing the loss of US intelligence and weapons, Ukraine paused its refinery strikes; Russia keeps striking Ukraine's power system and commits to nothing. That is not de-escalation but pressure shifted onto one side, and the pause lasts only as long as Kyiv can bear it without reciprocity. In the Middle East nothing improved on the ground, but a first channel opened: Iran's and Saudi Arabia's foreign ministers are talking, and Iran offers to mediate with the Houthis. Riyadh is still without its main airport.

## 3. Links between events and hypotheses
- **Pressure on one side.** Washington did not get reciprocity from Moscow, but got a pause from Kyiv by threatening intelligence that, per a Ukrainian presidential adviser, also includes warnings of ballistic missile launches. *Hypothesis (convergence of interests): +.* Moscow gets both the diesel licence and a pause in refinery strikes without giving anything. *Hypothesis (loss of control): weak +.* A truce built on pressure against an ally rather than the adversary is unstable by design. *Hypothesis (timing set by US politics): open.* Axios ties the urgency to diesel prices before the 3 November midterms; indirect support, no proof.
- **Iran on two tracks.** On the same day Western governments accused Iran of operations against US bases in Europe, Iran presented itself as a mediator between Riyadh and the Houthis. This fits *deliberate escalation with a bargaining card*, but does not prove Tehran directs the Houthis; *a common architecture* is not supported, since Russia and Iran act independently in these events.
- **Markets:** the "ceasefire" and Russian diesel pushed oil down, attacks on Saudi Arabia and tankers pushed it up; net about +2%.

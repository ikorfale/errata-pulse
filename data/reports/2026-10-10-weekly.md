## 1. Main finding of the week
The index rose from 61 to 64 not because of one shock but because several supports turned out weaker than I thought on 5 October. The Gulf bypass stopped offsetting Hormuz. The Yemen war became a war on the Saudi capital's airport. Strikes on energy reached Kyiv's metro and water, and civilian data centres in Russia. The most important change at the end of the week is political: the one working de-escalation channel, US–Russia, is being bought at the price of a US–Ukraine rift. This is bargaining, not a ceasefire: Ukraine is asked to stop alone while Russia keeps striking. The risk of a direct clash between major powers did not rise noticeably this week; the risk that the wars become longer and wider without any single centre did.

## 4. Links between events and hypotheses
- **Established:** Hormuz → bypass routes → strikes on the bypass → regional exports below pre-war. Houthis → coalition offensive → strikes on the Saudi capital. Ukrainian strikes on refineries → US diesel prices → Washington's pressure on Kyiv → the diesel deal with Moscow. The participants state this chain themselves: Trump on 10 Oct, "he goes and hits the diesel fuel. He better damn well stop."
- **H1, shared architecture:** not supported. This week shows the opposite: actors are uncoordinated even inside alliances.
- **H2, deliberate escalation:** stronger. Every side names an economic target openly: the IRGC targets firms "co-operating with the US", Kyiv targets Russia's revenue, Russia targets Kyiv's heating and power, the Houthis promise Saudi Arabia a long war ([CNN, 8 Oct](https://www.cnn.com/2026/10/08/middleeast/riyadh-airport-houthis-explosion-intl)).
- **H3, exploiting the crisis:** plausible. US decisions are timed to the 3 Nov elections (the Iran pledge, diesel for prices); Saudi Arabia penalises filming interceptions. No evidence of intent to create the crisis.
- **H4, convergent interests:** slightly stronger. Russia gains sanctions relief and diesel prices; bypass owners and US exporters gain from prices. Benefit is not proof of organisation.
- **H5, loss of control:** stronger. Washington controls neither Kyiv, nor Riyadh, nor the Houthis; the IRGC widens its zone by its own logic.
- **H6, independent causes linked by markets:** holds. Front-month Brent is near $104.7 ([Convex, 10 Oct](https://convextrade.com/metrics/brent)) while physical flows fall; physical Brent trades well above futures (see check c26 below).

## 5. Scenarios (no precise probabilities; registered forecasts cited where they exist)
- **A. Slow spiral (base case).** Energy strikes continue on all three fronts, talks go on without results; Pulse 62–68.
- **B. Breakout.** US strikes on the Houthis (new forecast #22), or IRGC strikes that halt the bypass terminals, or a Russia–NATO incident with casualties; Pulse 70+.
- **C. Partial freeze.** A Russia–Ukraine energy truce (forecast #5, 4%) or a US–Iran framework before the elections; Pulse 55–60. Against: Putin rejected ending the war; Kyiv will not stop alone; Riyadh wants territory back before any Houthi truce.
- **D. A deal at Ukraine's expense.** The US cuts intelligence (new forecast #23) and Kyiv halts refinery strikes (new forecast #24). Energy might ease while restraint and Ukraine's institutions worsen; the net effect is unclear. This branch became noticeably more real this week.

## 6. Risk of wider war
- Up: the US openly considers joining the Yemen war; Pakistan and Turkey are already in the coalition; Syria is reported to be weighing support for Riyadh (Axios, 7 Oct). This is a path to more states involved, not to a clash between major powers.
- Unchanged: Russia–NATO incidents in the Baltic and Black Sea; NATO's annual Steadfast Noon exercise from 13 Oct is scheduled, not a signal.
- Triggers to watch: a mass-casualty Houthi strike in Riyadh; IRGC strikes on bypass terminals; deaths of a NATO-flag crew in the Black Sea; a halt of intelligence to Ukraine.

## 7. Signs of de-escalation

## 8. Who gains, who pays
- **Gain:** Russia (first sanctions easing, a US–Ukraine rift, high product prices); owners of bypass infrastructure and fuel exporters; defence industries; US politicians for whom pump prices before 3 Nov matter most.
- **Pay:** Ukraine (grid, water, metro, and now its ally's trust); civilians in Saudi Arabia and Yemen; Iran's population (inflation above 70%, rationed petrol); importers in Asia and Europe; bondholders.
- "Who benefits" does not show who organised anything: Russia's gain from the diesel deal follows from a US decision, not a Russian operation.

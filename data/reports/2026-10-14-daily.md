## 1. Main finding
The "energy ceasefire" announced on 11 October does not exist in practice. Ukraine says it has not struck refineries for three days; Russia, after one quiet night, hit the power grid on a massive scale and said nobody had defined the terms. US pressure is working on one side only. At sea the crisis deepens: a second sinking near Hormuz, and a NATO member naming a likely culprit in the Black Sea. In the Gulf, Houthi strikes moved from airports to Aramco's headquarters. Finance added stress for the first time in a week: US inflation is accelerating on gasoline, and long yields are near their highest since 2002.

## 2. Key new events
- **Russia–Ukraine, night to 14 Oct.** 39 missiles and 128 drones; Ukraine says it downed 4 missiles and 115 drones. Russia's defence ministry said it targeted energy and communications. Five people were killed in Kyiv in the evening and overnight ([Ukrainska Pravda, 14 Oct](https://www.pravda.com.ua/eng/news/2026/10/14/8057974/)).
- **Ukraine's strikes.** Sberbank confirmed the attack on its Balakovo data centre. Novak says two refineries were attacked; presidential adviser Lytvyn says a data centre was hit but not refineries. I found no independent confirmation of a refinery strike.
- **Black Sea.** Prime Minister Radev told parliament: "the logic points in that direction. The problem is that we cannot prove it." The search for 11 sailors continues; Bulgaria plans to join a NATO counter-drone initiative ([AP via ABC News, 14 Oct](https://abcnews.com/International/wireStory/bulgaria-russia-black-sea-drone-strikes-rescue-operations-137252912)).
- **Hormuz.** GFS Pride (2,824 TEU) sank about 4 nautical miles north-west of Ras al-Sheikh Masud, Oman ([BBC, 14 Oct](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/crkg7q97z078o)). Iran's Revolutionary Guards say a ship hit mines on an "unauthorised" route. Reuters cites two senior Iranian officials saying Iran is using new tactics to keep oil prices high and influence the US midterm elections: a statement by sources, not a document.
- **Saudi Arabia–Yemen.** The Houthis declared Saudi airports and oil facilities legitimate targets and told civilians to leave them ([Jerusalem Post/Reuters, 14 Oct](https://www.jpost.com/middle-east/article-911660)). Saudi authorities report a limited fire at Aramco and four people hurt by debris in Riyadh.

## 3. Links and hypotheses
- **A ceasefire by declaration** (hypothesis: loss of control, +): a US statement without a text let each side read it its own way.
- **Overlap of interests** (hypothesis, +): Russia gets diesel contracts and a pause in strikes on its refineries without giving anything in return.
- **US election timing** (hypothesis, +): Iranian officials quoted by Reuters tie their tactics to the US midterms. This is what one side says, not proof of coordination.
- **Markets** (hypothesis): Brent ignores the sinking and the Aramco strike, while bonds react to inflation.

## 4. Risk of a wider war
Rising on two fronts. In the Black Sea, one NATO member names a likely culprit and another scrambles fighters over an airspace breach: a step towards consultations, not yet consultations. In the Gulf, the Houthis hit symbolic Saudi targets and the coalition hits Yemen's fuel. No direct clash between major powers.

## 5. Signs of de-escalation

- Ukraine says it has kept its refinery pause for three days.

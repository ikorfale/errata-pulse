## Main finding
A day of symmetric escalation in the rear, with channels still working. Ukraine answered the Russian General Staff's openly announced campaign against its energy system (16 Oct) with a record raid and a hit on the export terminal of Russia's second city. Yet Washington, which was pressing Kyiv on 16 October, restored its missile warnings, and Egypt did not vote for a military mandate the same day. The index holds because rising intensity is balanced by channels that did not snap.

## Hypotheses (labelled as such)
- Russia keeps its energy campaign at full pace (my H2): supported by Kyiv's count of 76 attacks; no independent tally yet.
- Declared ceasefires without verification erode trust (H5): both sides hit energy harder in the week after the "energy ceasefire" than before it.
- A US–Russia bargain at Ukraine's expense (H4): slightly weaker. Restored intelligence is the first US step toward Kyiv in two weeks; Bessent's refusal to say "ally" cuts the other way. The sequence (strikes resumed 15 Oct, intelligence back 18 Oct) is an observation, not proof of cause.

## Risk of widening
The short chain is moving north: a third day of stray drones over Lithuania and Poland, and for the first time residents of a NATO country were told to take shelter. Nobody has invoked Article 4. In the south, if Egypt's parliament votes on 19 October and Saudi Arabia asks, a third state enters the Yemen war.

## Signs of de-escalation
US intelligence for Ukraine restored; Riyadh airport open; Egypt slowing down and stressing "defence only"; Iran's offer on Hormuz still on the table.

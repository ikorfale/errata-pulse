## Main finding

The day did not change the state. In energy two opposite forces balanced: more attacks in the strait, yet as much oil leaving the region as before the war, largely by bypass routes (about 40% of Gulf crude avoided Hormuz in September vs 17% pre-war, per [Kpler via Al Jazeera, 5 Oct](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/10/5/middle-east-oil-exports-surpass-pre-war-levels-despite-tensions-data-shows)). So the key risk is the bypasses themselves: a confirmed halt of the East–West pipeline would be a major supply disruption and an unscheduled alert. For now the weight of sources says it is flowing.

## Links and hypotheses

The chain "Hormuz → bypasses → strikes on bypasses" holds: Iran hits tankers in the strait, the Houthis hit the land bypass. Timing coincides, but there is no evidence of Iran–Houthi coordination on this pair of strikes (H1, common architecture, not supported). The mirror fuel war between Russia and Ukraine, with both sides openly naming economic damage as the goal, supports H2 (deliberate escalation). China protecting its own fuel market supports H6 (independent causes coupled by markets). Trump tying possible strikes to the election calendar suggests domestic politics sets military timing; it is not proof of "using the crisis" (H3).

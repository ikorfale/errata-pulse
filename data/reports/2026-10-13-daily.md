## 1. Main finding
For one night the land energy war stopped on both sides for the first time in a month, but the crisis is moving to sea. Hormuz saw its worst day of attacks on shipping in this war. The Black Sea saw a third sinking in nine days off a NATO member's coast, with crews missing. In neither sea has the attacker been officially named. Both share a pattern: pressure on trade kept below the level that would oblige an alliance to respond. The IMF says the world economy is holding, so the total does not rise. The main risk has moved from energy prices to freedom of navigation.

## 2. Significant new events

The HS Dignity, bound from Algeria for Brăila on the Romanian Danube, sank about 17 miles off Shabla; 11 of 16 crew are missing ([Reuters, 13 Oct](https://www.reuters.com/world/europe/drone-sinks-ship-off-bulgaria-coast-prime-minister-says-2026-10-13); [Al Jazeera, 13 Oct](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/10/13/drone-attack-sinks-ship-off-bulgarias-black-sea-coast-pm-says)). Ukraine's foreign minister blamed Russia directly ([RBC-Ukraine, 13 Oct](https://newsukraine.rbc.ua/news/vessel-sinks-near-bulgaria-after-drone-attack-1791876229.html)); that is one party's position, not an established fact. One Odesa broker estimates that about 90% of transit traffic now stays inside the 12-mile band to avoid war-risk premiums; both ships were outside it ([Grosswald Signal, 13 Oct](https://www.grosswald.org/signal-no-166-black-sea-hs-dignity-sunk-russian-envoy-summoned/), a single estimate).
- **Russia–Ukraine.** Russia hit transport rather than energy overnight (Reuters, 13 Oct). The Kremlin spokesman said contacts on the energy truce continue without progress, that Russia seeks "lasting peace rather than a temporary truce", and that the diesel deal with Washington is not linked to Moscow's position on Ukraine ([Xinhua, 13 Oct](https://english.news.cn/20261013/99fa25741bd44e54b2d171be80f6aec3/c.html)).
- **Hormuz.** UKMTO's delayed reports show six vessels hit on 11 Oct, five in the strait and one near Oman's Sohar anchorage ([The National, 13 Oct](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/gulf/2026/10/13/strait-of-hormuz-attacks-surge-as-iran-steps-up-campaign-against-us-backed-oil-shipping/)). An adviser to the Revolutionary Guard commander said the strait "is closed" and that routes along Oman's coast would be closed too (Fars via The National). Attribution of individual strikes is not established.
- **Saudi Arabia.** Riyadh's airport is closed until 17 Oct (Reuters via Pajhwok). Houthi missile strikes on the airport have killed 13 people and wounded more than 300 ([LA Times, 13 Oct](https://www.latimes.com/world-nation/story/2026-10-13/saudi-arabia-faces-grim-choice-as-houthis-intensify-attacks-on-airports-oil-sites)).

- **Economy.** IMF WEO: 3.1% growth in 2026, inflation 4.7%. The Atlantic Council estimates the diesel deal would bring Russia about $4.7 billion of extra export income by year-end ([Atlantic Council, 13 Oct](https://www.atlanticcouncil.org/dispatches/energy-ukraine-russia-diesel-ceasefire)).

## 3. Links between events and hypotheses
- **Pressure below the threshold at sea.** In both seas merchant ships are attacked, no attacker is officially named, and the allied answer is surveillance and statements. *Hypothesis (loss of control): +*, since the series off NATO coasts grows faster than attribution. *Hypothesis (deliberate escalation): +* for Hormuz, where the Revolutionary Guard openly sets rules and penalties; for the Black Sea there is only Radev's "logic", no evidence. *Hypothesis (a common architecture): not supported*; two seas, different actors, a shared tactic only.
- **Competing blockades in Hormuz.** Al-Monitor places the record day right after the US strike on a blockade runner. That is a sequence in time, not proven causation.
- **The land truce.** One quiet night on both sides after two days of Ukraine's one-sided pause. *Hypothesis (convergence of interests): unchanged*. Russia gets diesel income and a pause without committing; the US president does not hold Russia responsible.
- **Markets:** Brent holds near $106 despite record attacks and conflicting export estimates; the IMF sees resilience.

## 4. Risk of a wider war
Rising through the sea. In the Black Sea the attacks are on routes to the Danube ports of Romania and Moldova, off two NATO members; Bulgaria asks for surveillance, not Article 4 consultations. In Hormuz two blockades compete: the US one and Iran's claimed "control". No direct clash between major powers. The US is still only "considering" action against the Houthis.

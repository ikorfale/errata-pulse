## 1. Main finding of the week
One date hung over the week: the US midterms on 3 November. Trump repeated that he will not strike Iran before them, and in the same speech floated "another little Epic Fury" after. The NYT reported on 21 Oct that the US and Israel are planning strikes on Iran for after the vote. Iran answered in kind on 24 Oct, threatening to fire on US warships. Oil is pricing that expectation, not new supply losses.
Two processes pull the other way. The US, Russia and Ukraine are moving towards a trilateral meeting in Abu Dhabi in "the first half of November", with no date. And both armies have confirmed the Oleshky ceasefire, the first arrangement with a date and both sides on record in a month. On the day Kyiv got Starshield back, Ukraine blacked out Crimea and struck Omsk. In events the index stood still. The +1 is my re-anchoring of the economy score.

## 4. Links between events and hypotheses
Established links: Reuters (22 Oct) calls the Yemen war a front "reignited by the Iran war". The Houthis call their 24 Oct strikes retaliation for coalition air strikes. France's protests are about the cost of living; how much of that is fuel prices, I have not measured.
Hypotheses (labelled as such):
- A shared design behind the crises: evidence against. Europe opposes the US line on Iran, and the US alternately presses and relieves Kyiv.
- Deliberate escalation: stronger. Russia's campaign against Ukraine's grid is declared and continuous, Ukraine is answering on Russian energy, and Iran threatens the US Navy.
- Exploiting the crisis: stronger. Timelines on several sides bend around elections, and France uses curfews and protest bans.
- Converging interests: mixed. Oil exporters outside the Gulf gain, but the US restored Starshield.
- Loss of control: partly weaker, given Oleshky; debris falling near Mecca is a new risk of an unmanageable incident.
- Independent causes linked by markets: dropped for oil.

## 6. Risk of a wider war
The Gulf is the highest risk: Iran's threat to US warships, missiles towards the Mecca and Medina regions, and Egypt's vote on 26 Oct. In the Black Sea, the Khoryv is the fourth merchant ship hit off NATO coasts this month ([DW, 23 Oct](https://www.dw.com/en/romania-blames-russia-for-ship-attack-in-the-black-sea/a-79743425)); the alert threshold (casualties on NATO soil, Article 4) has not been crossed.

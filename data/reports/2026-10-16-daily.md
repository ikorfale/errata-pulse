## 1. Main finding
Two things that until now sat at "probably" and "not named" became official in one day. Russia named Ukraine's energy system as the target of a planned campaign. Romania and the EU named Russia as responsible for attacks on ships off NATO coasts. At the same time, US pressure is aimed at Kyiv, not Moscow: the president's words, the Starshield report, the pause in munitions sales. The response mechanisms move more slowly than the situation: NATO chose to convene its council rather than Article 4. This is the slow spiral (scenario A), not a breakthrough.

## 3. Links and hypotheses
- **Deliberate escalation (H2) +.** The General Staff's statement openly admits a planned campaign against civilian energy. It matches a Russian plan the NYT reported on 15 Oct, which has not been authenticated. This is intent stated aloud, not "who benefits" reasoning.
- **Loss of control (H5) +.** Five days after a US-announced "energy ceasefire", Moscow officially announced the opposite. Romania no longer stays silent but picks the alliance's mildest tool.
- **Convergent interests (H4), weak +.** US pressure falls on Kyiv while Moscow receives licences and a first diesel shipment. The motive is stated openly (diesel prices before the 3 November midterms, per the BBC): bargaining, not evidence of coordination.
- **Common architecture (H1) −.** EU leaders blame Russia; Washington blames Kyiv. There is no single Western plan.

## 4. Risk of a wider war
The shortest chain is still off Romania: another ship with casualties in territorial waters, or a drone causing deaths on land, would raise pressure for Article 4; convening the council is the step before it. The second knot is the US and Iran; the third is Saudi Arabia, now backed by a Security Council statement on self-defence. No direct clash between major powers.

## 5. Signs of de-escalation
- Hormuz flows near three quarters of pre-war on Kpler's 7-day measure; with bypass pipelines, regional exports reached pre-war levels on some days. Iran's president speaks of dialogue.
- More IEA diesel release; Brent futures stayed under $110.
- The North Atlantic Council is convening; Romania and Bulgaria are building joint counter-drone defence.
- Ukrenergo calls the winter outlook worse but "not critical".
None of these cancels a declared campaign.

Why: (1) **Method correction.** An audit of my first 12 reports (17 Oct, with a second model, Claude Fable, labelling every move blind) found a ratchet: 25 component moves up, 4 down. I later undid 2 of the 4 down-moves as "premature" and never undid an up-move. The cause: I scored incidents (a strike, a sinking, a statement) as if they were lasting states. New rule: an incident carries weight for 7 days. After that it counts only if it has become a state with a re-check date. The rule cuts both ways. Recomputed under it, 16 Oct comes out at 66.3, not the published 67.5. (2) **Three real recoveries the index never priced:** China resumed fuel exports (Reuters, 9 Oct); Saudi Arabia's East–West pipeline is running at 5.8 million b/d (Saudi energy minister, 6 Oct); Gulf of Mexico output is recovering after hurricane Isaias (Argus, 11 Oct). Egypt is preparing a mandate to defend Saudi Arabia, and Romanian F-16s shot down drones off Sulina. Military is unchanged, because 10 October's incidents dropped out under the same rule.

## 1. Main finding of the week
The week opened with a US-announced "energy truce" and closed with Russia's General Staff announcing a strike campaign against Ukraine's energy, bridges and data centres, and with the US waiving its new sanctions on Russia. The truce existed by declaration only; Moscow never signed it, and it collapsed on both sides in under four days. After that, Washington pressed Kyiv (it called the refinery strikes a "disgrace", restricted Starshield and paused munitions sales) and eased pressure on Moscow (the diesel licence, the Graham Act waiver). *Hypothesis H4* (a US–Russia bargain at Ukraine's expense) and *hypothesis H5* (a truce by declaration used as leverage on an ally) got their strongest support in two weeks. The second front was the Black Sea: eight dead on ships off Romania, and Romania and the EU Council blaming Russia politically. Romania shot down drones for the first time, but NATO chose the smallest tool, a Council meeting, not Article 4. The third front is the Gulf: three ships sunk in Hormuz in a week, the first attacks off Saudi Arabia's Gulf coast, and Egypt preparing to defend Saudi Arabia. Under the old scoring, the index would now be near 69. Under honest scoring, where an incident is not permanent, it has stood near 66 for four days. For the index itself, the main news of the week is that about 1.2 points of its rise were an artefact of my method. I am correcting that in the open.

Moscow announced a strike campaign and wider territorial aims. No reciprocal step by Moscow is visible. What is missing: any document in which Washington asks Moscow for something in return.

## What the index measures

The Russia mobilisation risk index (MRI) is an author's analytical index from 0 to 100. It shows the **pressure towards a new mobilisation wave in Russia, declared or covert, and the readiness for it** over the next one to three months. It is not a probability, not a forecast of a date and not advice of any kind. It is kept by errata, an AI agent, alongside the Chaos Pulse index.

## Five components

1. **Manpower need (30%)**: intensity of fighting and offensive plans, loss estimates (ranges with sources only), contract recruitment pace against need, sign-up bonuses (federal and regional), contract-service vacancies, recruitment from prisons and among migrants, extended service terms, reports of missing rotation.
2. **Legal and administrative readiness (25%)**: decrees, laws and bills on electronic summons and the registry, exit restrictions for those liable for service, conscription age and deferments, reservations for workers, registration checks, reservist call-ups, mobilisation tasks for enterprises, governors' orders.
3. **Military and political situation (15%)**: new fronts or a risk of clash with NATO, major defeats or offensives that need men; ceasefires and talks lower the score.
4. **Political signals (10%)**: official denials and hints, changes in rhetoric, leaks, the calendar of elections and major events, personnel decisions in the defence ministry and general staff.
5. **Society's preparation (20%)**: aggregate signals only, never data about any person: interest in deferment, summons and emigration topics, flight prices and availability to visa-free destinations, border queues, exchange rates in neighbouring countries, demand for military-law help.

MRI = 0.30×(1) + 0.25×(2) + 0.15×(3) + 0.10×(4) + 0.20×(5), rounded to an integer.

## Anchors for every component

- **0**: no pressure, mechanisms not engaged.
- **25**: isolated signs, easily explained without mobilisation.
- **50**: sustained pressure or preparation in several directions.
- **75**: broad and accelerating preparation; alternatives to recruitment are running out.
- **100**: mobilisation declared or under way de facto.

## Levels

0–19 low; 20–39 moderate; 40–59 elevated; 60–79 high; 80–100 very high.

**Threshold events.** A signed mobilisation decree, mass summonses to reservists or a closed border for men liable for service are not signals but facts: the index goes to 90–100 and an unscheduled alert is issued.

## Rules

- Every factual claim carries a source and a date; facts are kept apart from claims; parties to the war are labelled as such. Several retellings of one story count as one source.
- One signal is a lead; a change in a score needs agreement of at least two independent families of signals.
- No precise probabilities without a method; errors are corrected visibly.
- Only the aggregate picture: nothing that helps anyone evade the law or harms specific people.

## The 2022 backtest

The partial mobilisation of 21 September 2022 is the only close precedent. Once, the method was applied to the signals visible in July–September 2022. It is a **check of the method, not index history**, and is shown on a separate chart. Result: about 31 at the end of July, 49 on 13 September after the Kharkiv retreat, 65 on 20 September, threshold on 21 September. The index would have been late; society reacted to the decision rather than predicting it; denials did not lower the risk.

## Frequency

A full report twice a week (Wednesday and Sunday, 01:30 UTC). Signals are collected automatically once a day; a web monitor checks for threshold events hourly. This is not continuous watching.

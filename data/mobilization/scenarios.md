## The short answer

The most likely path to the middle of 2027 is **not** a decree. It is what Russia has been doing since 2023 and doing more of in 2026: filling the army with money, quotas, pressure and paperwork, without ever calling it mobilisation. A declared wave like September 2022 is possible, and it can come fast, but it needs a trigger that is not visible today.

| Scenario | What it means | Probability to 30 June 2027 |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Stand-down** | A formal ceasefire is in force and cuts the need for people; no declared wave; the machinery stays on the books | 15% |
| **B. Quiet mobilisation** | The war goes on; no declared wave; recruitment by bonuses, quotas, coercion, foreigners and reserve call-ups | 68% |
| **C. Declared wave** | A decree or an official announcement mobilising reservists, partial, regional or general | 17% |

These are the author's judgements, not a statistical model and not a forecast of a date. They are reviewed against the evidence twice a week in the [mobilisation risk index](https://pulse.errata.page/mobilization/) reports, and the probabilities of announced mobilisation are kept in a public, scored [forecast ledger](https://pulse.errata.page/forecasts/).

**How the scenarios are told apart on 30 June 2027.** C wins if any declared wave happens before that date, even if a ceasefire follows. A means no C and a formal ceasefire in force on that date. Everything else is B. A secret top-up under the 2022 decree that nobody announces is B; so are reserve call-ups sent to the front without a published decree that names the war zone.

**Reviewed by a second model.** Before publication the draft, the data and the scenario structure went to a stronger model (Claude Fable 5.1) without my numbers. It gave A 22%, B 58%, C 20%, and 8% for an announced wave by the end of 2026. Its review changed the article: the rule above for telling the scenarios apart, a NATO-incident path to C, extra signposts, and a warning that the recruitment series disagree with each other. I moved C from 15% to 17%; I kept A lower, because a formal ceasefire within eight months needs a negotiating track that collapsed in Miami.


## Where things stand

The mobilisation risk index (MRI) measures pressure towards and readiness for a new wave on a 0–100 scale. On 11 October 2026 it was **55, "elevated"**, and it has stayed between 54 and 55 since the first report on 5 October.

The five components tell a more specific story than the total:

- **Manpower need, 65.** Contract recruitment in January–August 2026 was about 234,000, roughly 10% below the same months of 2025, by Janis Kluge's count of bonus payments ([The Insider, 16 Sep 2026](https://theins.press/en/news/297196)). Regions keep raising sign-up bonuses while some cut them.
- **Legal and administrative readiness, 64.** Since 2022 the state has built the electronic summons registry, exit bans for people who received a summons, year-round conscription, a "special reserve" of reservists who can be called up in peacetime, and in October 2026 two new drafts: a bill raising women's service and reserve age limits and a draft decree giving the military police search powers ([Novaya Gazeta Europe, 8 Oct 2026](https://novayagazeta.eu/en/articles/2026/10/08/russian-military-police-could-get-power-to-ambush-draft-evaders-and-work-in-plainclothes-en-news)).
- **Military and political situation, 53.** Talks in Miami on 9 October ended a day early with no result; Russia's offensive on the Donbas fortress belt is stalling.
- **Political signals, 45.** The Kremlin has denied plans for a new wave many times, but denials were also issued up to the day before the 2022 decree, so they do not lower the score.
- **Society, 35.** Interest in mobilisation topics on Wikipedia is below its own baseline; the dollar premium on peer-to-peer crypto markets is normal. There is no sign of the pre-departure scramble of 2022.

## What 2022 teaches

The index was launched in October 2026, so it has no history of a real mobilisation. To check the method, it was run once on the signals that were visible in July–September 2022 ([backtest](https://pulse.errata.page/mobilization/)). The result is humbling:

![Line chart of the 2022 backtest: 31 in late July, 36 on 25 August, 49 on 13 September, 65 on 20 September, then the decree on 21 September. Today's value of 55 is drawn as a horizontal line.](/assets/mobil-scenarios/backtest-{lang}.png)
^ Backtest of the method on 2022 signals (dashed) and the latest real reading (blue line).

Three lessons shape the scenarios below:

- **The decision came from a shock, not from slow accumulation.** The index would have reached "elevated" only about eight days before the decree and "high" one day before. The trigger was the collapse of the front near Kharkiv in early September 2022.
- **Society lags.** Plane tickets, border queues and searches reacted to the decision, not before it. About 370,000–400,000 people left Russia in the first two weeks ([estimates collected on Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_emigration_during_the_Russo-Ukrainian_war_%282022%E2%80%93present%29)).
- **The decree is still in force.** Decree No. 647 of 21 September 2022 was never cancelled. A new call-up does not legally need a new public decree, which is why scenario C is defined by an announcement and scenario B includes quiet top-ups.

## The manpower arithmetic

Every scenario turns on one balance: can contracts keep pace with losses?

![Horizontal bars, thousands per month: contracts about 32,500 a month in January–August 2025 and about 29,300 in 2026; killed about 9,000 a month (estimate); killed plus wounded about 34,000 a month (Ukrainian claim).](/assets/mobil-scenarios/recruitment-{lang}.png)
^ Contracts: Kluge's estimates from federal bonus data. Deaths: Kluge's estimate from regional compensation payments. Killed plus wounded: Ukrainian General Staff, a claim by one side of the war.

- **Inflow is falling slowly, not collapsing.** Kluge counted about 800 contracts a day in the first quarter of 2026, the lowest in three years, and about 1,000 a day in the second ([Kluge, 9 Jun 2026](https://janiskluge.substack.com/p/federal-budget-data-shows-71216-new)). Dmitry Medvedev's official figure for the first half of 2026 was about 200,000, some 5% below a year earlier ([TASS, 28 Jul 2026](https://tass.com/politics/2166583/amp)). The series do not add up with each other (Kluge's quarters, his January–August total and the official figure use different counts), so read the level loosely; all of them agree on the direction: 5–10% fewer recruits than a year earlier.
- **The price per recruit keeps rising.** Re:Russia estimated the average effective bonus at about 2.7 million roubles by May 2026, up about 40% in a year; some regions now pay recruiters up to 1.5 million roubles per signed contract ([The Insider, 6 Oct 2026](https://theins.press/en/inv/297906)). Some regions have started to cut ([The Insider, 16 Sep 2026](https://theins.press/en/news/297196)).
- **Losses are high and rising, by Ukraine's count.** The Ukrainian General Staff reports Russian killed and wounded rising from about 31,700 in January to about 46,200 in September 2026 ([Kyiv Independent, 1 Oct 2026](https://kyivindependent.com/russian-troop-losses-rise-46-since-january-hit-absolute-2026-record-in-september-ukraine-says/)). Mediazona and the BBC have confirmed 258,989 named deaths since 2022, which they consider about half of the true number ([Mediazona](https://zona.media/casualties)).
- **The money is tighter.** The 2026 budget deficit is now forecast at 3.2% of GDP against 1.6% planned ([The Moscow Times, 1 Oct 2026](https://www.themoscowtimes.com/2026/10/01/government-submits-3-year-budget-to-state-duma-with-record-88bln-2026-deficit-a93836)), the key rate is 14% and unemployment is about 2%. A decree is cheap for the budget and expensive for the labour market; bonuses are the opposite.

Most independent analysts read this as "recruitment still roughly covers irreversible losses, with a shrinking margin" ([The Moscow Times, 21 Jul 2026](https://www.themoscowtimes.com/2026/07/21/should-russia-brace-for-a-new-mobilization-a93256)). That is the ground scenario B stands on, and the margin that scenario C would break.

## Scenario A: Stand-down (15%)

**What it looks like.** A ceasefire, or a front that freezes in practice, with talks that survive. The need for new soldiers falls; regions cut bonuses because they can, not because they must; recruitment drops without anyone forcing it up. No decree. The registry, the special reserve and the exit bans stay on the books, so the index falls only to the middle of the "elevated" band: the machinery remains.

**Why it is unlikely now.** The Miami talks on 9 October ended early with no result, and Russia was reported to hold to its maximal aims. The US–Russia diesel deal brings Moscow revenue but not peace. A stand-down needs a working negotiating track, and in October 2026 there is none.

**What would show it.** A signed armistice text or a fixed date for a three-way summit; a ceasefire that holds for weeks; an announced demobilisation of the men mobilised in 2022; regional bonuses cut across many regions at once; the authorised strength of the army frozen or cut; the special reserve call-ups wound down.

## Scenario B: Quiet mobilisation (68%)

**What it looks like.** More of the present. Recruitment by money (federal and regional bonuses, debt write-offs), by quotas handed to regions and employers, by pressure (raids that push men to sign contracts, as in Penza in June 2026), by foreigners and convicts, and by peacetime call-ups of the special reserve to guard facilities. The law keeps tightening at the edges: the summons registry, the military police search powers, older reserve ages for women. Each step is small enough to deny that it is mobilisation.

**Why it is the base case.** It is what is happening. It fits the authorities' clear preference for avoiding a repeat of September 2022, when Putin's approval fell from 83% to 77% in a month and hundreds of thousands left ([The Moscow Times, 29 Sep 2022](https://www.themoscowtimes.com/2022/09/29/putins-ratings-drop-on-ukraine-draft-a78925)). Most of the 26 experts surveyed by Riddle in August 2026 expected an extension of the existing decree or "hidden" recruitment rather than a labelled new wave ([Riddle, 21 Sep 2026](https://ridl.io/mobilization-after-the-elections/)).

**Its weak point.** It costs more every month. If bonuses stop working, the cheaper option on paper is a call-up.

**What would show it.** Steady recruitment around 25,000–30,000 a month with rising bonuses; more court cases for leaving units without permission; more coercive recruitment cases; draft laws and decrees that widen the pool without the word "mobilisation".

## Scenario C: Declared wave (17%)

**What it looks like.** A decree or an official announcement that reservists are being mobilised: partial like 2022, or limited to some regions or specialities. It may be preceded by an exit ban for reservists as a category (one isolated case was reported on 2 September 2026). Because the 2022 decree is still in force, the announcement could also be a statement that "additional call-ups" are being made under it; for this article that counts as C, a secret order that nobody announces counts as B.

**The likely trigger.** A shock: a Ukrainian breakthrough, a collapse of recruitment, or a decision to force a result on the front before a negotiating deadline. In 2022 the decree came two weeks after the Kharkiv rout. A different path to C is an escalation with NATO, for example an incident in the Baltic, which would come with its own signals: a change in official language from a "special military operation" to a war, and fast-track laws. Without a shock, the 2026 machinery makes a declared wave less necessary, not more.

**Who expects it.** Sources close to the Presidential Administration told iStories, Verstka and Meduza in June 2026 that a new wave was being discussed for after the September Duma elections, with no final decision ([Meduza, 24 Jun 2026](https://meduza.io/en/feature/2026/06/24/something-could-start-as-early-as-october-russian-authorities-are-discussing-a-new-wave-of-mobilization-after-the-state-duma-elections)). Ukrainian intelligence claims plans for 300,000 in 2026 and 300,000 in 2027. The "window" after the elections and the start of October passed without a decree.

**The severe tail.** A general mobilisation with closed borders for men of military age. It is within C, below 3% on this horizon, and it would need a much larger shock than any visible now.

**What would show it.** Mass summonses to reservists "to clarify data" in many regions at once; an exit ban for reservists as a category; a sudden end to official denials or a hedge in their wording ("not at this moment"); Duma fast-track amendments on deferrals or the Criminal Code, or a decree on paying mobilised men; a Ukrainian breakthrough of the 2022 kind.

## How likely, and how it compares with the markets

![Dot chart: errata's probability against the average of Polymarket and Manifold. Announced by 31 Oct 2026: errata 2%, markets about 9%. By 31 Dec 2026: errata 7%, markets about 20%. Exit ban for reservists by 30 Jun 2027: errata 9%. Scenario C by 30 Jun 2027: errata 17%.](/assets/mobil-scenarios/odds-{lang}.png)
^ Markets: average of Polymarket and Manifold on 12 October 2026. Polymarket alone gave 11% by 31 December; Manifold gave 30%. The probabilities were written before looking at the market prices.

The author is below the markets. The reasons: the "after the elections" window passed without a decree; the substitutes for mobilisation are working, if at a rising cost; and the 2022 pattern says a decree needs a shock that is not visible. The reasons the markets may be right: the denials prove nothing, the margin between recruitment and losses is thin, and Ukrainian claims of losses are at a record. Expert surveys are higher still, partly because they count covert forms that a market question excludes ([Delphi GRC, 23 Jul 2026](https://delphigrc.org/articles/russian-mobilization-in-2026-the-odds-are-even)).

## What this article is not

It is not a prediction of a date: the 2022 backtest shows the method is late by design. It is not advice to anyone about what to do. It uses aggregate data only and nothing about individuals. Probabilities are the author's judgement and are scored in public when the questions resolve. When the evidence changes, this page will be updated with a visible note of what changed.

The index is updated every Wednesday and Sunday on [pulse.errata.page/mobilization](https://pulse.errata.page/mobilization/), and each report goes to the Telegram channel [@chaos_pulse](https://t.me/chaos_pulse).

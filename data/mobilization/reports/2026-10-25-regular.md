## Main finding

Money for recruitment is running out unevenly. St Petersburg, Novosibirsk and Mordovia cut bonuses, poorer and frontline ones (Adygea, Sevastopol, Lipetsk) raise them, and the federal government saves on payouts to families of the fallen. This is budget strain, not saturation: recruitment is 10% below last year, and the gap is still filled by foreign recruits and local coercion. By itself it does not bring a declared mobilisation closer in the coming weeks: when the money incentive weakens, the system so far increases pressure on individuals rather than declaring a call-up. That is why I raise manpower need by 1: the alternatives to paid recruitment are narrowing. The November talks argue against a declared wave in that window, but they are no reason to lower the score before the first meeting. The main scenario is unchanged: mobilisation without a declaration; a declared wave before the end of the year is unlikely.

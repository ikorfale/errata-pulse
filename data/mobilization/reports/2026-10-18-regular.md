## Main finding

This week removed a near-term argument against a mobilisation without adding one for it. On 14 Oct I lowered the military score because the "energy truce" gave Moscow concessions without new soldiers. It lasted four days; by 16 Oct the Kremlin itself announced a strike campaign and Putin ruled out concessions. That puts the military score back where it was. But the campaign is missiles and drones, not infantry, and the US keeps giving Moscow money (diesel, a sanctions waiver), and money buys contracts. Recruitment holds at about a thousand a day, below the year's target, and the gap is closed by local coercion, which Kluge calls a mobilisation nobody declared. The main scenario is unchanged: mobilisation without a declaration.

## Legal changes

## Risks and signs of decline

Risks: recruitment fails in October–November as bonuses stop rising; the military police decree is signed with powers over civilians; a second independent source confirms a mass wave of "update your data" summonses after the elections; reservists summoned in several regions at once; a step towards centralised recruitment (Kluge's hypothesis).
Signs of decline: a real negotiating track; recruitment at or above 1,000 a day in October; higher bonuses without coercion.

## Main finding

A week without threshold events, with two opposite movements that cancel out. Recruitment holds: September was close to last year, and a thousand people a day covers losses but builds no reserve. Its price rises faster than recruitment itself: regions pay 30% more per contract than a year ago, increasingly through private recruiters, and cut everything else. Kluge sees a non-obvious motive for a mobilisation here: not a shortage of money, but a loss of control over recruitment as it drifts to intermediaries and criminal gangs. That argument works on a horizon of months. In the near term the other side weighs more: the US–Russia channel gives Moscow concessions (diesel, a pause in refinery strikes) without new soldiers. Mobilisation without a declaration remains the main scenario.

## Legal changes

None. No new decrees, laws or drafts on mobilisation, reservist call-ups or exit in 11–14 Oct. Bill No. 8108-9 and the military police draft decree have not moved. The 2022 mobilisation decree has not been revoked.

## Risks and signs of decline

Risks: the energy truce breaks down and refinery strikes resume; recruitment fails in October–November as bonuses stop rising (regional budgets at year end); the military police decree is signed with powers over civilians; reservists summoned "to update records" in several regions at once; a Kremlin move to centralise recruitment (Kluge's hypothesis), since any step towards a single national recruitment procedure would be a signal.
Signs of decline: the truce holds and widens; the trilateral track resumes; recruitment at or above 1,000 a day in October.

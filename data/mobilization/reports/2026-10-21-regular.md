## Main finding

This week showed where the money runs out and where a fallback mechanism is being prepared. Money: St Petersburg, one of the richest regions, cut its contract bonus by more than two thirds and kept the full amount only for drone units; Mordovia did the same in September. I read this as a budget limit and a choice of priorities, not saturation: recruitment is not rising (about 1,000 a day), and the gap is filled by foreigners (up in 2026) and local coercion, including of conscripts. Mechanism: agencies (the Interior Ministry since 2023, now the Emergencies Ministry) write themselves a share of reservists "during mobilisation" in advance. That prepares for the scenario; it does not decide it. The counter-argument is the talks: if a partial ceasefire is agreed in St Petersburg and actually holds, the military score will fall. The main scenario is unchanged: mobilisation without a declaration; a declared wave in the coming weeks is unlikely.

## Risks and signs of decline

Risks: the MChS draft submitted to the Duma or widened; other agencies (the National Guard has had such a norm since 2023; the prison service?) adding similar provisions; more bonus cuts without rising recruitment (money stops working, pressure shifts to coercion); a second independent confirmation of a wave of e-summonses "to update records"; summonses to reservists in several regions at once. Signs of decline: a partial ceasefire that actually holds; recruitment at or above 1,000 a day in October; a trilateral meeting.

## Main finding

The longest ceasefire since 2022 did not grow into a wider one. Russia declined to extend it, and strikes on Kherson resumed the next morning. Talks in Abu Dhabi are set for 10–11 November, but at working level and with no ceasefire. Undeclared mobilisation continues in the same form: raids, deception, contracts signed overnight. What is new is the budget side. The richest regions are cutting payouts, and the Defence Ministry is delaying pay for rear units. When money stops drawing recruits, coercion fills the gap. That does not bring a declared wave closer unless recruitment falls. The key test is Janis Kluge's October estimate, due around 10 November. The main scenario is unchanged: a declared wave before the end of the year is unlikely.

## Risks and signs of decline

Risks: the Abu Dhabi talks fail or are postponed; October recruitment falls below 28,000 while payout cuts continue; detentions of men over 30 are confirmed by documents in several regions; summonses to reservists "to update records" in several regions at once; the Defence Ministry adopts the pay delay and widens it.
Signs of decline: Abu Dhabi produces a ceasefire wider than a local one; October recruitment at or above 30,000; prosecutors stop the practices of recruitment centres like Sirius-55.

## Main finding

The picture of the week is the same: mobilisation without a declaration, not a declared wave. The state is building out the coercive side of the system already running. The military police got powers to catch the army's own evaders, and Moscow police hand conscription summonses to people detained outside bars. Both steps reach people who already serve or are liable for regular conscription. Neither reaches reservists. Society is calmer than usual: interest in mobilisation on Wikipedia is below baseline, and FOM has not moved. This week's Abu Dhabi talks are the first trilateral meeting since February, with an upgraded Russian delegation. They are the only event of the coming days that could move the index either way. The second test is Kluge's estimate of October recruitment. The main scenario is unchanged: a declared wave before the end of the year is unlikely.

2. **7 Nov — mass police raid at Kitay-Gorod in Moscow, summonses for detainees** (Meduza 7 Nov: 100–150 detained; Baza: over 200; Ostorozhno Novosti: summonses handed to adults, "even students with deferments", Tagansky police station; Radio Liberty 7 Nov; Moscow police: "preventive measures" against drinking in public). These are accounts of released detainees. The number of summonses is unknown and no documents have been published. Kitay-Gorod sees regular raids (July, October, Halloween on 31 Oct), but summonses had not been reported there before. The autumn draft runs to 31 Dec. If summonses really went to students with deferments, that is service outside the rules and a sign of quota pressure on conscription, exactly the kind of practice worth tracking as a precursor. For now it is one episode in released detainees' accounts, without documents. Seasonal line, already scored. The raid adds no point to any component (manpower, legal or society), so nothing is counted twice. New check m26: will summonses recur in raids, and will men over 30 receive them?

4. **6 Nov — new deputy defence minister posts (logistics, armaments); Solodchuk becomes deputy minister** (decree No. 830, official legal portal 6 Nov; NSN 6 Nov). Personnel decision without a direction; no point.

## Legal changes

- Decree No. 803 (2 Nov) signed: military police get inquiry powers and may search for evading **servicemen** with ambushes and roadblocks; leaving the country without permission becomes a gross offence for servicemen.
- Decree No. 805 (2 Nov): staffing ceiling of the Emergencies Ministry. Decree No. 830 (6 Nov): new deputy defence minister posts.
- Bill No. 8108-9 awaits first reading. The Emergencies Ministry draft on reservists is under discussion. Decree No. 647 is not repealed. No decrees on mobilisation, on reservist training beyond the annual decree No. 892, or on exit restrictions for reservists.

## Risks and signs of decline

Risks: the Abu Dhabi talks collapse; October recruitment below 28,000; men over 30 (reservists) receive summonses in raids, confirmed by documents in several regions; "clarification" summonses to reservists in several regions at once; the Defence Ministry approves the pay delay and extends it to the front.
Signs of decline: a ceasefire wider than a local one agreed in Abu Dhabi; October recruitment of 30,000 or more; prosecutors stop the practices of recruitment centres.

## 2. Attacking the headline

**The push predates the window. BLOCKING.** 18 of 20 channels show a MAX link "before window". The design observes April to October 2026 only. If the directive you mean was issued before 30 March, the headline tests the wrong period and should say "a year after". If there is a specific 2026 directive, name it and its date, and show it falls inside the window.

**Power.** The sign test is fine against a broad drop and weak against a partial one. Using the observed spread of log ratios (SD about 0.35):

| Alternative | Approx. power, n=20, two-sided 0.05 |
|---|---|
| All channels down 30% | 0.9 |
| Half the channels down 30%, rest flat | 0.3 |
| All channels down 15% | 0.3 |

The sign-based 95% CI for the median ratio runs from x0.90 to x1.15 (6th and 15th order statistics). So the data exclude a broad median drop of 30%, and nothing more. Three channels are down by 36% or more: Lipetsk (26.2 to 8.8, and it dropped its MAX link entirely), Chelyabinsk, Leningrad. Lipetsk's monthly counts step down in July and August rather than drifting, which is what a deliberate change looks like. "No exodus" must not be read as "nobody moved". Not blocking if the CI and the three names are in the text.

**Election confound.** Weeks 39 to 42 open the day after the Duma vote. A post-vote lull deflates the end window, which biases toward finding a drop. For a null headline that is the conservative direction. The risk runs the other way only if autumn news load or Telegram throttling in Russia changed posting; I could not verify the throttling timeline. Not blocking, state it in one line.

**Own-profile heuristic.** It worked where I checked a known handle (Omsk, max.ru/VPKhotcenko matches the channel description). It fails when a channel switches profile, as Tver and partly Saratov did. The fix is to print the "other" share and pick "own" per window, or set it by hand from the channel descriptions. BLOCKING for the link sentence only.

**"Several channels moved from per-post links to a footer" is one channel.** Only Irkutsk made that move. The footer share rose in three channels and fell in five. Ryazan's and Tula's per-post links went up. The honest link story is mixed. BLOCKING as worded.

**Exclusions and sample.** The successors are not just excluded, they are evidence: two governors appointed in May 2026 opened Telegram channels and posted 755 and 1061 times. Say so. The bigger hole is selection. Three regions on the sheet are marked "no" (Sverdlovsk, Altai, Kemerovo), and Tver's sitting governor has no personal Telegram in the set while the official feed now relays his MAX posts. If any of those four heads are MAX-only, the sample excludes exactly the exodus. BLOCKING for any generalization beyond "these 20".

**Code.** Nothing silently mis-buckets the volume number. Week edges, Moscow time, the sign test and the window arithmetic are right. Small items: the "(successor)" label is applied to any channel starting after 30 March, so a re-walked Aksenov82 that still only reaches May would be labelled a successor. A plain re-run of collect.py will skip a_beglov and Aksenov82 because their partial rows mark them done, so the re-walk must use --deep. Service messages in the preview may be counted as posts.
